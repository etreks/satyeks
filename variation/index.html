Notes on Variation, Emergence and Universe.

Using a hypothetical universe with inverted electromagnetic forces, the article examines how different rules might prevent or enable complexity. It proposes that stability, life, and intelligence may not be predetermined goals, but outcomes that emerge when simple rules unfold over time.

08:06

1. The Mirror Universe

The starting question: what if electromagnetism were inverted?

Instead of opposite charges attracting and same charges repelling:
Positive attracts positive.
Negative attracts negative.
Positive repels negative.

A small simulation was built around this idea. Immediately, a strange consequence appeared.

Atoms could not form, because electrons would run away from nuclei rather than orbit them.

No atoms. No chemistry. No molecules. No water. No DNA. No life. No thought.

The universe appeared to lose its ability to build complexity.

This led to a deeper intuition: the atom is a small disagreement that never fully resolves. Complexity seems to emerge from tensions that persist rather than tensions that immediately resolve.

2. Why These Laws?

This raised a bigger question: why does this universe have these particular laws?

Why opposite-attracts-opposite? Why not same-attracts-same? Why not some entirely different interaction?

The first intuition was not design. The first intuition was evolution — not biological evolution specifically, but the logic underneath it: variation, persistence, time.

3. The Evolution Analogy

A thumb is a useful example. Nature did not consciously decide “let’s create a thumb and see if it works.” Nature did not have a hypothesis. Nature did not have an objective. Nature did not have a destination.

A mutation occurred. Most mutations disappear. This one happened to increase functionality within a particular environment. The mutation survived. The thumb was not planned. The thumb persisted.

The important distinction: these anomalies are not conscious. They are mutations. They are random. Selection comes afterward.

4. Extending the Logic to Universes

What if physical laws themselves are variations? Not because we know they are, but because the structure of the explanation is similar.

Imagine countless possible rule sets — different electromagnetic behavior, different dimensions, different constants, different interactions.

Most may produce nothing interesting. Some may collapse. Some may never generate stable structures. Some may generate stars. Some may generate chemistry. Some may generate life.

Not because they were designed to — simply because those particular configurations persisted.

5. Stability Is Not the Purpose

People often assume stability is the objective. But why?

Evolution does not aim at stability. Evolution does not aim at intelligence. Evolution does not aim at humanity. Evolution does not aim at anything.

It generates variation. Some variations survive. Some disappear.

The question is not “why stability?” The question is “why did stability survive?” That feels like a more interesting question.

6. Intelligence Is Not the Goal

Intelligence is not the purpose of the universe. Consciousness is not the destination.

Evolution explores possibility space — it does not optimize toward a predetermined endpoint.

Humans are not necessarily the peak. Consciousness is not necessarily special.

Current intuition: we are simply one of the strange structures that happened to emerge when the rules of this universe were run for 13.8 billion years. Nothing in that statement requires purpose. Nothing in that statement requires intention.

7. The Big Bang and Energy

The early universe was unimaginably hot. Atoms could not exist. Eventually it cooled. Atoms formed. Stars formed. Everything else followed.

But the deeper questions remain — where did all that energy come from? Why was the universe hot to begin with? Why is space expanding? Why is it still expanding? Why do these rules exist at all?

Physics can often describe what happened. It becomes less certain when asked why these particular rules exist.

8. Simple Rules, Complex Outcomes

Maybe reality starts with only a small number of fundamental rules. Then time does the rest.

Run those rules long enough and complexity emerges — galaxies, stars, planets, life, thought.

This reminds one of Conway’s Game of Life. Simple rules. Unexpected outcomes. No designer. No destination. Just interactions unfolding over time.
